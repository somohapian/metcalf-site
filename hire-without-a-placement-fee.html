How to hire without paying a placement fee

A placement fee is what a contingency recruiter charges when you hire someone they introduced. If you find and hire someone yourself, you can avoid that fee. You’ll still need someone to find candidates, read applications and make time for interviews. Here’s how to decide whether that work fits your week.

What will it cost to make the hire ourselves?

Think through one opening, from writing the job description through the accepted offer. Estimate each person’s hours for the whole search and multiply by their hourly cost. Add job ads, software and any background checks you’ll pay for. Use the same role and time period when you compare that total with outside help. Include the interview time your team would still spend either way.

Where should we look first?

Write down what the person needs to do in their first month, what the job pays and when they’ll work. Put that on a careers page. Share the link with employees and people who know the work. If replies are thin, reach out to people with the experience you need before you spend more on ads. Find qualified candidates.

What should we do when someone applies?

Give one person responsibility for replies and set aside time to read applications. Ask candidates about a piece of work they’d actually do in the job. Write down what you heard, compare it with the requirements and tell each person when they’ll hear from you. Plan your interviews.

How do we get from the interview to an accepted offer?

Agree on pay, schedule and start date with the person you want to hire. Put the terms in writing and give them time to read and ask questions. Once they accept, tell the other finalists and make sure the new hire knows where to go on their first day. Prepare the offer.
