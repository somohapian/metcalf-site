HIRING GUIDES · THE STACK METHOD

Practical hiring help for small businesses.

Find qualified candidates, run useful interviews, make competitive offers, and keep good employees. Start with the problem you need to solve.

Where is hiring getting stuck? Too few suitable applicants calls for a different response than candidates dropping out or offers being declined. Look at what is happening before deciding what to change.

Before you rewrite the job ad, raise the salary, or add another interview, look at where the search is getting stuck. Choose the topic below that matches your situation. Each guide explains what to check and gives practical steps you can use yourself.

Five parts of hiring to look at, from attracting candidates to keeping good employees.

Finding qualified candidates

When the right candidates can't find your job, or won't apply when they do.

  • Why aren't qualified candidates applying to my job?
  • How do I write a job title that candidates actually search for?
  • How does a small business compete with bigger companies for talent?
  • Where should I be posting jobs that aren't Indeed?

What should a small-business careers page include? Use the five-point checklist to review yours.

Talking with candidates

When the conversation goes quiet, your process stalls, and the good ones accept somewhere else first.

  • Why do candidates ghost us before the interview?
  • How fast do I need to respond to applicants?
  • What does a good first message to a candidate look like?
  • We're losing candidates between interview and offer. Why?
Keep the conversation going →

Interviewing and choosing candidates

When the resumes look right, the interviews go well, and you still can't tell who will do the job.

  • Why do I keep making bad hires even when interviews go well?
  • What's the difference between a structured interview and a regular one?
  • Should I be using a scorecard for hiring?
  • How do I assess for skills I don't have myself?
Ask better interview questions →

Making job offers

When offers get rejected, counter-offers win, and your top choice walks at the eleventh hour.

  • How do I make a competitive job offer?
  • What do I do when a candidate gets a counter-offer?
  • How much salary negotiation room should I leave?
  • Why do candidates accept and then back out?
Prepare a competitive job offer →

Keeping good employees

Keep your people productive, satisfied, and engaged. Stay connected, make work better, and follow through, for new hires and the people already on your team.

  • Why do good employees leave?
  • How can a small business improve employee retention?
  • What should I ask in an employee check-in?
  • How do I help a new hire settle in?
Improve employee retention →

Choose one thing to improve first

Look at where people leave the process. If suitable applicants are scarce, check the job description, pay, careers page, and where you are advertising. If people disappear after an interview, review the conversation and the wait for a decision.

Make one practical change, give it an owner, and decide what you will measure. That might be suitable applications, days waiting between interviews, accepted offers, or feedback from new employees. Review the result before changing everything at once.

These guides are organized as Source, Talk, Assess, Close, and Keep: the STACK Method. Use whichever part helps with the work in front of you.
