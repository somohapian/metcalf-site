Keep · Conversation guide

Make your next employee check-in useful.

A regular conversation about the work, the experience of doing it, and what would help next.

Start while things are going well

Keep, our approach to employee retention, is useful for a new hire or someone who has been with you for years. Ask two questions: is the work going well, and is the job working well for the person doing it?

Stay connected. Recognize good work and ask what makes the role worthwhile. Make work better by discussing useful support, learning, or changes to the working arrangement. Follow through by returning to what each person agreed to do and asking whether it helped.

Use page one to prepare from your own perspective. Use page two to hear the other person and record any accepted actions. If things are going well, preserve what works. If something needs a closer look, use the worksheet for a specific concern.

No account is needed. Nothing is sent or shared automatically. Keep your copy and decide what to discuss or share. Bring it to the next conversation if you want to return to earlier notes.

Manager: Based on what you have seen, how is the work going? Where could your support be most useful?

What is working and worth keeping?

What work is worth recognizing, and what did it make possible? What makes this role worthwhile and should be preserved?

Make work better

What is worth discussing or preserving? Pick up to two, most important first.
Workload or priorities · Tools or information · Skills or practice · Communication or teamwork · Recognition · Growth · Schedule or working arrangements · Pay or benefits · Something else · Nothing specific today

For growth: What would worthwhile progress look like? Learning something new or continuing to do the current role well are both useful answers.

Follow through: what remains open?

If you have earlier notes, bring them. What happened with your commitments? Did the action help, and what still needs attention?

Nothing specific today is a complete answer. Recognize what is working without inventing a problem. Use page two for the conversation and any next steps.
