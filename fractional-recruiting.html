What Fractional Recruiting actually is

Fractional recruiting gives a business an agreed share of recruiting support without adding a full-time employee. At Metcalf Search, that support runs on a flat monthly subscription. Your recruiter works with your managers on the roles in scope, and the fee does not increase when you hire someone or negotiate a higher salary.

When hiring gets difficult, a business might pay an agency for individual placements or hire its own recruiter. Fractional recruiting offers another option: ongoing support sized to the workload. The right choice depends on how often you hire, which roles you need to fill, and how much of the work your team can handle.

Why the model exists at all

The idea is familiar: get experienced help for the amount of work you have.

A business may use a fractional CFO when it needs financial leadership without enough work for a full-time finance chief. Recruiting can work the same way. You may need someone to plan and run searches throughout the year without needing a recruiter on payroll full time.

A per-hire agency can make sense for an occasional search. With recurring hiring, compare those fees with the cost of ongoing support and consider who maintains the process between openings. The number of hires alone will not settle it: scope, timing, and the roles involved matter too.

The practical question is how much recruiting work you need someone to own, and how consistently you need them.

Every hiring model, defined

Buyers usually compare fractional recruiting against whichever model they used last. It helps to see the whole field. There are eleven common ways a business gets hiring work done, and each one has a cost structure, a natural buyer, and a failure mode.

Ad-hoc DIY hiring. The owner or hiring manager posts the job, reviews applicants, and runs interviews. You keep direct control and avoid a recruiter fee, but still need to budget time, advertising, and tools.

Offshore sourcing. An overseas team researches candidates and handles outreach. Confirm the market knowledge, quality standards, and who will screen responses and manage the rest of the search.

Virtual assistant. A remote assistant can handle posting, scheduling, and inbox coordination. Recruiting experience varies, so agree which decisions and candidate conversations remain with your team.

AI recruiting tools. Software that screens, ranks, or messages candidates automatically. Useful as an accelerator inside a working process. It cannot diagnose a broken one, and it is not a substitute for someone accountable for the outcome.

Full-time internal recruiter. An employee dedicated to your hiring. This can fit a sustained workload. Budget salary, employer costs, tools, and the time needed to manage the role.

Fractional recruiter. A senior recruiter embedded at a flat monthly fee, working across the roles in your agreed scope. Built for ongoing but sub-full-time hiring volume. Breaks down if you genuinely only hire once every few years.

Contingency agency. Paid when you hire a candidate the agency presents. The agreement sets the fee, often as a percentage of salary, along with any replacement or refund terms. Compare those terms for each provider.

Staffing agency. Supplies temporary or temp-to-hire workers, with its costs and margin included in the bill rate. It can help with temporary coverage. Check the rate, minimums, and any conversion fee before using it for a permanent hire.

Contract recruiter. A recruiter hired for a defined period or workload, often billed hourly or under a project agreement. Agree who owns the records, what happens at the end, and how the work will be handed over.

RPO. Recruitment process outsourcing means a provider takes responsibility for some or all of the recruiting process under contract. Scope, staffing, minimums, and pricing vary. It overlaps with some fractional services but is not a synonym for a part-time recruiter.

Retained search. A firm paid in installments to run a dedicated executive search, whether or not it closes. The right instrument for a confidential C-suite hire. Priced accordingly, and not meant for filling a coordinator role.

What fractional recruiting costs

Metcalf Search uses a flat monthly subscription. The tier depends on concurrent roles and the level of onsite support. Within that agreed scope, the fee does not change with the number of hires or their salaries. Other providers may price their services differently, so compare the agreement as well as the monthly number.

The useful comparison is not the monthly number in isolation. It is the cost of each option at your actual hiring volume and scope. This example assumes twelve months of recruiting support and six hires at an average salary of $65,000, with a 25 percent contingency fee.

Contingency agency, 6 hires at 25 percent of $65,000$97,500 / yr
Full-time internal recruiter, $95,000 salary plus about 30 percent payroll load$123,500 / yr
Plus recruiting tools and job board spend for that recruiter$8,000–$15,000 / yr
Recruiting Partner, one active role at a time, $2,950 × 12 months$35,400 / yr
Recruiting Partner Plus, two to three concurrent roles, $5,950 × 12 months$71,400 / yr

If your scope calls for Recruiting Partner Plus for all twelve months, the $71,400 subscription is $26,100 below the $97,500 contingency example. Choose the tier by concurrent roles and scope, not annual hire count alone. Salary, payroll load, tools and agency fee assumptions vary, and these figures exclude vacancy costs.

At the same salary and agency fee, two contingency hires cost $32,500, which is $2,900 less than twelve months of Recruiting Partner. Three cost $48,750, which is $13,350 more than that base tier, provided one active role at a time fits your hiring plan. The number of months you need support also changes the comparison.

A full-time internal recruiter becomes easier to justify as the work approaches a full-time workload. Fractional recruiting fits the space between occasional searches and a permanent recruiting seat. Compare the work you need covered, the roles running at once, and the total cost.

Include recruiting tools and advertising in the comparison, along with the cost of leaving a role open. Coverage, overtime, and delayed work can matter as much as the recruiter's fee. Metcalf Search includes recruiting tools and some job advertising through Talent Points in its plans. Use your own costs when comparing that with an employee or another provider.

Who this is for

Our model is designed for businesses with roughly ten to two hundred employees and recurring hiring needs. Look at the number of roles open at the same time, the work involved, and the help available internally. A manufacturer, law firm, and brokerage may need similar recruiting steps, but each search still needs role-specific knowledge.

The clearer signal is behavioral. A fractional partnership tends to be the right call when referrals have stopped producing candidates, when roles sit open past sixty days without a real shortlist, when the owner or a hiring manager is personally reading resumes at night, or when you have written an agency check in the last eighteen months and felt it clear the account.

It is usually too early when the company is under ten people and the owner both is the hiring process and prefers it that way, or when hiring happens once every few years and there is nothing to build a rhythm around.

When fractional recruiting is the wrong choice

A monthly service needs enough recurring work to justify its cost. These are situations where another option may fit better.

You hire once every two or three years. There is nothing for a monthly partnership to do between searches. Pay a contingency fee when the need appears and keep the money in the business the rest of the time.

You need one confidential executive hire. A single C-suite search with discretion requirements is what retained search exists for. Different instrument, different price, right tool.

You need temporary coverage at short notice. A staffing agency may be a better fit. Ask about worker availability, required skills, total bill rates, and any minimum assignment or conversion terms before choosing a provider.

Your role requires a deep specialist network you cannot buy generally. Some niches, certain clinical specialties and some regulated engineering disciplines among them, are genuinely better served by a boutique that has spent a decade in that one lane.

Nobody internally has time to interview. A recruiter can fill the top of the funnel and run the process, but somebody at your company still has to meet the finalists and decide. If that person does not exist yet, fix that before hiring anyone to feed them candidates.

How a partnership runs

The first week is the part most owners want described concretely, so here it is.

Activation. A tier is chosen and the partnership starts. A kickoff call is scheduled within 48 hours.

Week one. Kickoff covers role intake, comp bands, the profile of who has succeeded in the seat before, and the interview panel. Active sourcing begins during that call. A branded careers page goes up if you do not already have one worth using. Within five business days of the kickoff, the first slate of vetted candidates is in front of you.

Week two. First interviews typically start. Every candidate on that slate was screened by a person before your calendar got touched.

Ongoing. Weekly search cadence per open role, candidate communication handled between interviews, debrief structure kept moving, offer construction and closing support through the start date.

Between searches. This is where a fractional partnership separates from an agency. Downtime goes into building talent pools for roles you know are coming, improving the careers page, running the referral program, and doing stay interviews with the people you already have so the next opening is one you chose rather than one that happened to you.

An illustrative example

Illustrative comparison

Illustrative example, not a client result. Consider a fabrication shop that needs to replace its plant manager while also hiring a project coordinator. Here is how the owner could compare the options.

With a contingency agency, compare the proposed placement fee and replacement terms with the needs of this search. Ask what work the agency will handle and what happens if the hire does not stay.

With a full-time recruiter, look beyond these two openings. Is there enough recruiting work across the next year to justify a permanent position, and who will manage that person?

With a fractional partnership, check that the tier covers both concurrent roles and the support each manager needs. A recruiter already working with the business can use what they have learned, but the new opening still needs an intake and a search plan. No model guarantees a particular filling date or retention result.

How we help with each part of hiring

Every search gets a plan for finding candidates, an agreed schedule for interviews and updates, consistent screening questions, and support preparing the offer. We also help managers plan the first ninety days and continue checking in with new hires and existing employees about their work, support, and reasons to stay.

Common questions about fractional recruiting

What is fractional recruiting?
Fractional recruiting gives your business ongoing recruiting help for part of a recruiter's time. The recruiter works with your managers to find candidates and run the hiring process. At Metcalf Search, it is a flat monthly subscription with a scope based on the number of roles and onsite support you need. It can fit a business that hires regularly but does not have enough recruiting work for a full-time employee.
What is a fractional recruiter?
A fractional recruiter works with your team on an agreed share of the recruiting workload. That can include defining the role, sourcing and screening candidates, coordinating interviews, preparing offers, and supporting the new hire after they start. Ask each provider who will do the work, how much time is included, and which steps remain with your team.
Is fractional recruiting the same as fractional talent acquisition?
The terms overlap, but they do not guarantee the same service. Fractional talent acquisition may include longer-term workforce planning as well as filling roles. Embedded recruiting describes how closely a recruiter works with your team. RPO, or recruitment process outsourcing, means contracting out some or all of a recruiting process and can use different staffing and pricing arrangements. Compare the actual scope, named recruiter, time commitment, fees, and cancellation terms.
How is fractional recruiting different from a contingency agency?
A contingency agency earns a fee when you hire a candidate it presents. The agreement sets the fee, often as a percentage of salary, and any replacement or refund terms. A Metcalf Search subscription pays for ongoing recruiting work each month, whether or not a hire starts that month, with no fee added when you hire. Compare both options against your expected searches and the support you need between hires.
How is fractional recruiting different from hiring a full-time internal recruiter?
A full-time internal recruiter is your employee and works on your hiring full time. You provide the salary, employer costs, tools, and management. A fractional recruiter provides an agreed level of support without adding a full-time employee. Metcalf Search includes recruiting tools and team support. Full-time can make sense when the workload is consistently large enough to keep that person busy.

You can move between tiers at a monthly cycle. Compare scope, onsite time, and terms on the pricing page.
What kinds of businesses is fractional recruiting for?
Our model is designed for businesses of roughly ten to two hundred employees with recurring hiring needs. It is worth considering when the owner or HR manager is spending too much time sourcing, screening, and scheduling, but a full-time recruiter would be difficult to keep busy. The work can span industries. We also check for conflicts with existing clients before agreeing to a search.
What does an embedded recruiter actually do day to day?
An embedded recruiter works alongside your team to write job descriptions, find and screen candidates, schedule interviews, keep applicants informed, and prepare offers. At Metcalf Search, the work also includes support after the hire starts. Between searches, we can work on talent pools, the careers page, referrals, and retention within the agreed scope.
How quickly can a fractional recruiter start?
We schedule the initial kickoff call or meeting within 48 hours of activation. Active sourcing begins during the kickoff call itself.
